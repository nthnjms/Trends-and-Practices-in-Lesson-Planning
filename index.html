01 — The Study

Why this study?

Lesson planning is where a pre-service teacher's training turns into a plan for a real classroom. It is also where many struggle most. Knowing the frameworks is one thing; building lessons whose objectives, activities, and assessments hold together is another.

Planning is also changing. AI tools, digital platforms, learner-centered approaches, and competency-based expectations all place new demands on teachers, and the preparation offered by teacher education programs is not the same from one institution to the next.

This study asks how English pre-service teachers in Eastern Visayas plan their lessons, what they make of these changes, and how their institutions' preparation shapes both. Their experiences come first, in interviews. A survey built from those interviews follows, and a statistical method called structural equation modeling then examines how the pieces fit together.

Information

Please read this before deciding whether to take part. You are welcome to ask questions at any point (see Contact).

Purpose of the study

The study looks at how English pre-service teachers plan their lessons, how recent developments such as AI tools, digital technologies, learner-centered approaches, and competency-based planning fit into that work, and how institutional preparation relates to lesson planning competence. It aims to inform recommendations for strengthening lesson planning preparation in English teacher education.

Who may participate

You may take part if you are:

  • enrolled in a Bachelor of Secondary Education program majoring in English, or an equivalent program, at a participating institution in Eastern Visayas;
  • currently doing, or have recently completed, Field Study or Practice Teaching; and
  • willing to take part voluntarily and give your consent.

For the interviews, we also ask that you have submitted at least three formal lesson plans, so that you have enough planning experience to talk about.

What participation involves

Interview. One individual conversation, in person at your institution or by video call (for example Zoom or Google Meet). You may answer in English, Filipino, or your regional language. With your permission, the conversation is audio-recorded. You may be asked to describe a lesson plan you have recently submitted, but you will not be asked to hand it over.

Survey. A questionnaire in which you rate how much you agree with statements about your lesson planning and your preparation. It is completed on your own, online or on paper, and opens at a later stage of the study.

Time commitment

An interview takes about 45 to 60 minutes. The survey is expected to take about 20 to 25 minutes. [Confirm final time estimates]

Taking part is voluntary

You decide whether to participate. You may skip any question, stop at any time, and withdraw without giving a reason. Whether or not you take part will have no effect on your grades, your practicum evaluation, or your standing at your institution. Invitations are not made through your own course instructors or practicum supervisors.

A small token of appreciation is planned for participants. It does not depend on what you say. [Confirm details]

Possible risks and benefits

The risks are small. Some people may feel a little uncomfortable when reflecting on difficulties in their planning. You can decline any question. There is no direct personal benefit, but your responses will help inform recommendations for improving lesson planning preparation in teacher education.

Confidentiality and how your information is handled

  • Your name is replaced with a code when interviews are transcribed and when survey responses are entered.
  • Audio recordings, transcripts, and survey data are kept in password-protected storage accessible only to the researcher and authorized members of the research team.
  • Results are reported as themes or group-level findings. Nothing that identifies you will be published.
  • Information is handled in line with the Data Privacy Act of 2012 (Republic Act No. 10173).
  • Data are kept for at least five years after the study is completed, then permanently deleted.

How your information will be used

What you share will be used only for this study: for the thesis, and for any related presentations or publications. Interview themes will be used to build the survey. Information will not be shared with anyone outside the research team without your written consent and ethics committee approval.
